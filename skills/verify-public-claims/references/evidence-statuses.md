# Evidence Statuses

Apply the narrowest status that matches the evidence available on the review date.

## `supported`

Use when a direct source supports the full material meaning, scope, tense, and attribution. A source can be genuine but still fail to support the exact wording.

## `qualified`

Use when evidence supports a narrower version. Add the missing boundary, such as a date, geography, plan, sample size, observed period, source attribution, or dependency.

## `inference`

Use when multiple facts reasonably suggest a conclusion that no source states directly. Present it as analysis or interpretation, never as a reported fact.

## `unsupported`

Use when evidence is absent, inaccessible, circular, unrelated, too weak, or contradicted by a stronger source. Testimonials and examples do not establish universal performance.

## `stale`

Use when the claim is time-sensitive and the available evidence cannot support present-tense wording. Date the claim or obtain a current source.

## Source Discipline

Prefer sources in this order when they directly address the claim:

1. Current official records, documentation, datasets, or first-party statements.
2. Original research, standards, transcripts, or direct creator material.
3. Credible reporting that identifies its evidence.
4. Expert commentary used for interpretation, not sole proof of a disputed fact.
5. Aggregators, reposts, and unattributed summaries only as leads to stronger sources.

Record publication date and event date separately when they differ. Treat search snippets as discovery aids, not final evidence. Keep quotations short and necessary.
